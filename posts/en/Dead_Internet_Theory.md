## Or How There's Nobody on the Other Side: The dead internet theory

![There's nobody on the other side](../static/images/dead_internet.png)

If you're a person who interacts with people on the internet, more than once it will have happened to you that they replied with things that had nothing to do with what you were saying, that they tried to steer the conversation down other paths, or that you simply thought the other person behind their monitor is just dysfunctional.

How would you react if I tell you there isn't always someone behind a screen, and that for sure more than once you've argued with a robot? Well, that same face you're pulling right now is the one I had when I discovered the [Dead Internet Theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dead_Internet_theory). For many it's nothing but a fantasy, but with the amount of videos circulating of giant bot farms and social media accounts, it's getting more and more obvious.

[![A random bot farm, there are a thousand videos like this one](../static/images/granja_bots.png)](https://www.youtube.com/shorts/qz0k79aW0o4)

A few years ago, maybe even decades, buying bots to have them _follow_ you on social networks like twitter or instagram was pretty common. In fact, many of those who today are influencers by follower count got there by paying for advertising (on youtube or twitch it's pretty obvious when someone pays, since the video comes up for all of us even if we don't watch that topic) or got there by paying companies that fill your social networks with bots and interactions. At first, it was nothing more than a number of ghost users populating your posts. Likes here, retweets there... But the **algorithm has changed and the creation of ragebaits has made that evident.** Social media companies bill by traction and interaction. **It's better to have 300 people arguing and generating a lot of replies than 5000 hitting like without saying anything.**

This has been taken in by the big bot companies, who little by little have been refining their creations. Right now, with LLMs and the supposedly AI, we can make our ghost accounts do and say things, even post stuff on the internet so the account looks more real. [It's estimated that 51% of internet users are bots](https://reporterzy.info/en/5234,dead-internet-theory-is-a-fact-bots-now-outnumber-people-online), which makes it more than likely that you've read bot replies, seen bot photos or videos, or even argued with some bot spouting nonsense to generate traction on that slightly hot news item.

One of the great paradoxes of this situation is that robots need users to feed their algorithms and know how to behave. They have to know what to say when someone is accusing them of something on the internet. And they have thousands of examples to be able to answer, since there's nothing more human than arguing about trivia with someone on the internet. But since right now more than half the users are robots, what's happening is that robots are feeding on robot content, and answering with things a robot would answer. They can keep going for a while yet until the [**"data poisoning"**](https://genai.owasp.org/llmrisk/llm042025-data-and-model-poisoning/) takes effect, and by then I'm sure they'll have found a solution.

{marginnote}Talking with robots that are insistent and chase you with their line of argument, even when they're wrong, is Turing's wet dream.{/marginnote}

Another of the great paradoxes, and the one that gives this theory its title, is that the tool that was made to connect us all has disconnected us even more and put us to talking with robots. The technological advance that internet represented was cursed and now only serves for other robots to goad you into writing that they're confused or that they're right, over whatever stupid thing they've said.

# The dead company theory

When you work at a company long enough you can come to recognise the way some of your colleagues have of writing or expressing themselves. And it's more than evident when the one writing is another person. It's not the first time it happens to me in an email thread that you ask for certain credentials, making a mention of that person who solved your problems before and with whom you're on closer terms, and that they reply to my email with a text that has a lot to do with the subject we're dealing with. Very similar to what I said at the beginning about interactions on the internet, and that after talking with them in private, they get it done in a matter of seconds and without any complication, and answer by email in a more familiar manner. This has led me to be pretty suspicious of emails and of the people who, supposedly, are behind the screen.

Talking with colleagues or friends one realises that there are more and more people who **hand the email chain over to an llm and ask the AI to give them an answer for that email chain.** What's more, I've developed an application exactly for that same purpose at one of my clients. And it was an internal tool, not even for customer support. All of this leads me to conclude that little by little we're entering the **Age of the Dead Company.** A company that has decisions taken from the data, data collected on the internet, on social networks and by everyday users. Some of them are bots, let's remember. Those decisions go through a translation process into projects, in large part by the project owners or by the upper layers of the chain, who lean on AI to take decisions when reading their data, and those projects are managed as a whole by workers who use AI to develop their product. All of this seasoned with doubts, requests, emails, conversations and meetings carried out with AI agents and answered by AI agents.

The companies that make up the **Dead Company Theory** are those that have workers as middlemen between Artificial Intelligences. Workers whose job can be substituted by the same technologies they use, and workers who bring nothing at a productive level beyond presenting the results that the chatbot of the moment has spat out at them, or the excel cells that their digital assistant has moved or redone. These **dead companies** make up the problem of giving the green light to the Falling Tendency of the Rate of Profit, which has been kept at bay for years by economic policies that fought against it, but little by little it's winning again until it causes another crisis again.

This type of companies doesn't necessarily have to look for the maximum performance per worker, nor the biggest economic profit. It's a natural evolution of using Artificial Intelligence for everything related to problem solving, and running human interactions through the AI agents, so they filter and look for the best communication between peers. It doesn't come dictated from above, nor does it look for the elimination of workers as intermediaries between the raw material and the manufactured final product or the commodity (that would be a whole other theory). This theory understands as natural the succession of events that took place in technological industrialisation and the constant disappearance of people behind the screen. It's not that there are bots where there used to be job positions — those too — but that the people who keep holding their job position are the pinch of salt in the different outputs of Artificial Intelligence. **Behind those screens there are bots, and behind those bots there are people.** This makes it so that, mainly, communication happens between bots and has a small personal layer in each of the outputs.

{marginnote}Work is being done with bots, in projects generated by bots, analysed by bots, with technical doubts and resolutions managed by bots, and used in the last instance by bots.{/marginnote}

All of this sounds alarmist, I know. But think about it:

Juan gets an email from Alberto with a request to fix certain problems in the corporate excel where they keep the company's expenses throughout the year. This email, Juan, he runs it through an AI that tells him what he needs, and once he knows, he tells another AI to fix what they're asking for. Once the AI has fixed it, he tells the first one again to write a corporate email for Alberto explaining what he's done and how he's solved it, which is going to be received, copied and pasted into another one of Alberto's AIs to know if what has been done is right. All of this, in a project that came out of a need studied in data, data extracted by an AI, and studied by an executive, Santiago, who used an AI to tell him what the needs of his company were.

In this example we have the interaction of 3 people (made up so it's simpler), Juan, Alberto and Santiago, who have been translating and forwarding each other things that their artificial intelligences have told them. They have been making the AIs talk to each other, through human mediation, but the humans have printed little or none of their own labour into the projects. We still find certain digital artisans who manipulate the excel by hand, read the email chains and work on their digital tools. But there are more and more of those who prize quantity over quality, primacy over craftsmanship, and there's no going back. There's no real added value to artisanal solutions when they're digital. It hardly matters if an application underneath has taste, is made by hand and the _very best good practices_ of programming or business ethics have been applied. After all, the end user or the little boss of the moment is only going to see the surface, the topmost part of the process, and is going to turn that work into data, into interactions, into euros per minute.

![The aesthetics of AI is already another topic](../static/images/terminal_ai.png)

Maybe in the future, the dead companies will be able to work with the chinese car factory where they don't turn the lights on. Absolutely dark and zombie companies, that generate products for humans to use, consume and appreciate.

Or not.
